Realmweaver · for storytellers

How your campaign fits together

You don't need to know anything about data modeling to use Realmweaver. The app thinks about your campaign the same way you already talk about it: a world you build, a story you plan, and what actually happens at the table. This page walks that picture from a glance to the full map — go exactly as deep as you like, and stop whenever you have what you need.

Level one · a glance

Your campaign is a story in three layers

Everything you make in Realmweaver lives in one of three layers, and you already know them — they are my world, my plan, and my table nights:

The world — the setting you build: the people, places, organizations, objects, and lore. The story — what you plan to happen: adventures broken into scenes, plot threads you're tracking, and the secrets you're waiting to reveal. The table — what actually happens: game nights, your players' heroes, the fights, and your quick notes.

One well-worn path through those layers, if you want one (start anywhere — this is a path, not a rule): set the stage → sketch the world → plan the story → open plot threads → plant secrets → prep the session → play, then close the loop.

In the monastery story: you write a line about the Ashen Vale, sketch the monastery and the abbot, plan “Whispers Below”, open the thread What is the Vigil hiding?, plant the secret, prep Friday — and after Friday, the recap and loose ends feed the next one.

Level three · the full model

The full model, for those who want it

Everything below is the same picture, at full depth: the four kinds of connection, the web of mentions, the caretakers that quietly keep it all true, and every field on every type. Where an engineering term exists, it sits in a small tag after the plain words — take it or leave it.

The four kinds of connection

Every line on the map above is one of four kinds. (There is also one true act of containment: an adventure contains its scenes, in order, and a session's log holds its archived fights.)

Kept in step on both sides bidirectional sync

You set one side; the app writes the other. These never drift apart.

  • An NPC belongs to a faction; the faction has members. Assign Maren to the Vigil and he appears in its member list — move him, and both lists update.
  • A location sits inside a bigger location, and the bigger one knows its parts.
  • An article nests under a parent article, and the parent knows its children.

One thing remembers another one-way links

A simple pointer, held on one side. The rest of the app finds it whenever it needs to.

  • A faction is led by an NPC, and keeps its headquarters at a location.
  • A location is controlled by a faction.
  • A scene takes place at a location and features NPCs.
  • A session plans scenes, NPCs, and locations, and records how each plot moved — advanced, stalled, or unchanged.
  • A secret was revealed in a session.
  • A clue supports a revelation — a secret pointing at the bigger secret it helps uncover.
  • A fight remembers the session and scene it began from, and is archived into the session's log.

Links that carry their own words labeled links

Some connections are half link, half prose — the words are yours.

  • An NPC has named relationships with NPCs and player characters — you write the label ("rival", "old flame", "family") and the story behind it.
  • A location connects to other locations — each road, tunnel, or hidden stair described in your words.
  • A history entry says what happened to an NPC or location, and can point at the session or article where it happened.

The web of mentions untyped links, resolved by scanning

The loosest and most powerful kind: any-to-any threads that hold your world together.

  • Anything mentions anything — type an @ in a description, and both sides remember it. NPCs, locations, factions, articles, plots, and scenes can carry mentions; items and adventures can be mentioned too.
  • An article can reference anything; a plot involves anything; a secret concerns the people, places, and things it is linked to.
  • A running-log entry tags whatever it names, and you can pin up to fifteen things for quick reach.

Backlinks: the web, read backwards

None of those threads is ever stored twice. When you open something, the app scans your whole campaign for every thread pointing at it and shows them as backlinks — so they are never stale, and a mention follows a rename, because it tracks the thing itself, not its name.

The quiet caretakers

Three pieces of housekeeping run on every change, so you never have to think about them:

Both sides stay true bidirectional sync

Membership and nesting are written on both sides in the same breath, every time. There is no "sync" button because there is nothing to sync.

No place can contain itself cycle guard

Try to make the Ashen Vale sit inside the Crypt while the Crypt sits inside the Ashen Vale, and the app refuses — the change is rejected and everything stays exactly as it was. The same guard watches the lorebook's nesting.

Nothing is left dangling cascade deletion

Delete anything, and one sweep clears every thread that pointed at it — memberships, casts, plans, secrets, relationships, mentions, pins. A history entry that pointed at a deleted session is the one gentle exception: it keeps its words and simply becomes a manual entry, so your timeline survives.

Two more comforts in the same spirit: a duplicated campaign is truly its own — copy a campaign and every thread inside the copy is rewired to the copy, so a what-if branch never reaches back into the original. And when something still looks off, the Continuity Checker reads the whole web for you: broken links, circular nesting, orphaned pieces, contradictions.

Every field, on every type

Open only what you're curious about. Each card lists what the type holds, how it connects, and where the monastery story touches it.

NPCamber · Sidebar → NPCs
What it holds

Name, description, personality traits, backstory, motivations, the secrets they keep to themselves, freeform stats, an example quote in their own voice, and voice notes — how they sound when you play them: accent, cadence, the phrases they lean on.

How it connects

Belongs to a faction. Has named relationships with NPCs and player characters, each with your own label and description. Keeps a history of what has happened to them — each entry can point at the session or article where it happened. Can @-mention anything.

In the monastery story

Abbot Maren — traits: serene, patient, evasive. Example quote: "The lower stair is closed. It has always been closed."

Locationemerald · Sidebar → Locations
What it holds

Name, description, its own secrets, and points of interest — each with a notice difficulty, a read-aloud description, and investigation checks and interactions, each with an outcome. Hidden loot, where each piece can hide at one of those points of interest. A history timeline, like an NPC's.

How it connects

Sits inside a bigger location. Connects to other locations, each connection described in your words. Is controlled by a faction. Can @-mention anything.

In the monastery story

The Hidden Crypt sits inside The Ruined Monastery sits inside The Ashen Vale. A loose flagstone — notice difficulty 15 — hides a tarnished censer your players keep walking past.

Factionviolet · Sidebar → Factions
What it holds

Name, description, goals, alignment, resources, and influence.

How it connects

Has members and is led by an NPC. Keeps its headquarters at a location, and controls territory — the locations that name it as their controller. Can @-mention anything. Rivalries with other factions live in your prose, where they belong.

In the monastery story

The Order of the Silent Vigil — goal: keep sealed what the founders buried. Led by Abbot Maren; headquartered at the Ruined Monastery.

Itemsky · Sidebar → Items
What it holds

Name, description, a rarity from common up to artifact, and properties in your words. Optionally a kind — weapon, armor, potion, scroll, wondrous, tool, or other — plus attunement, weight, and value.

How it connects

It points at nothing — the rest of the world points at it. Mention it in lore, let a plot involve it, let a secret concern it, and open it to see all of that as backlinks.

In the monastery story

The Reliquary of Ash — very rare; cold to the touch; hums near consecrated ground.

Lorebook Articlecyan · Sidebar → Lorebook
What it holds

Title, a category — lore, history, or cosmology — and the article text itself.

How it connects

Nests under a parent article, building your encyclopedia's tree. Can reference anything in the campaign, and can @-mention anything in its text.

In the monastery story

"The Vigil's Founding" nests under "A History of the Ashen Vale", and mentions Abbot Maren, the crypt, and the reliquary.

Adventureorange · Sidebar → Storylines
What it holds

Title, a hook, a theme, and a level.

How it connects

Contains its scenes, in order — the one true act of containment in the whole model.

In the monastery story

Whispers Below — hook: pilgrims have gone missing near the ruined monastery. Three scenes: an arrival, a descent, and what sleeps below.

Sceneblue · inside its adventure
What it holds

Title, a kind of moment — combat, social, exploration, or puzzle — and a status: planned, in progress, or completed. Read-aloud text for your players, private GM notes for you, skill checks — each naming the skill, the difficulty, and what it reveals — and rewards.

How it connects

Takes place at a location and features NPCs. Can @-mention anything. A combat scene is your prep; the live fight is its own thing at the table.

In the monastery story

"What Sleeps in the Crypt" — a combat scene at the Hidden Crypt, featuring Abbot Maren. Read-aloud: "The air below is colder than the stone should allow."

Plotyellow · Sidebar → Plots
What it holds

Title, description, and a status — active, dormant, or resolved.

How it connects

Involves anything in your world — NPCs, locations, factions, items. Each session records how it moved: advanced, stalled, or unchanged. Can @-mention anything.

In the monastery story

"What is the Vigil hiding?" — active. Involves Maren, the crypt, and the reliquary. After Friday: advanced.

Secretno accent · Sidebar → Secrets & Clues
What it holds

Title, the secret itself, a category — secret, clue, revelation, or rumor — whether it has been revealed, and your private notes. A revelation can be marked vital — one the mystery can't afford to miss — with its own count of how many clues it wants pointing at it (three, unless you say otherwise). One type still covers all four; the only machinery mysteries add is a single optional thread from a clue to its revelation.

How it connects

Concerns the people, places, and things it is linked to. A clue can name the revelation it supports, and the tracker shows each revelation how many clues point at it. When the moment comes you mark it revealed, and it remembers which session revealed it.

In the monastery story

"Maren sealed the crypt himself" — concerns Abbot Maren and the Hidden Crypt. Revealed in Session 3, when the flagstone finally moved.

Session Logrose · Sidebar → Session Timeline
What it holds

A status — planned, active, or completed — the session date, and when the night actually started. Before play: the adventure it draws on, planned scenes, a curated cast of NPCs and locations, and the plot threads in play. During play: running notes; a timestamped log whose entries can tag anything and are stamped with what kind of moment they were — a note you typed, a scene change, a fight, a new NPC, a dice roll; a beats checklist of little moments you hope to hit; and the dice rolls themselves. After play: the recap, notable events, loose ends, a player-facing recap, how each plot moved, and every archived fight.

How it connects

Plans scenes, NPCs, and locations. Its running log records events as they happen. Records how each plot moved. Fights are archived into it when the night ends. The Session Prep Wizard builds one; the Session Runner runs it; the Session End Wizard closes it.

In the monastery story

Session 3 — plans the three scenes and casts Maren. The log records "they found the loose flagstone" at 9:42. The crypt fight is archived inside. Plot: advanced. Loose end: nobody asked about the raven.

Player Characterteal · Sidebar → Player Characters
What it holds

The player's name, and the character sheet: background, species, personality, appearance, backstory, ideals, bonds, and flaws; class and level, ability scores, actions, and eighteen skills, each tracked from untrained up to expertise.

How it connects

It points at nothing — the rest of the world points at it: NPCs keep named relationships with it, and mentions reach it. When combat starts, it steps into the fight as a combatant.

In the monastery story

Serra Duskwalker, played by Kim. Abbot Maren keeps a wary named relationship with her — "she asks the right questions."

Noteslate · Sidebar → Notes
What it holds

Title, the text, free tags, and when it was created and last edited.

How it connects

Deliberately connected to nothing, so you can jot without filing. Still taggable, pinnable, and searchable.

In the monastery story

"The players loved the talking raven — bring it back."

Live combatno accent · Sidebar → Combat Tracker
What it holds

The round, whose turn it is, and the combatants — each one of your players' characters, an NPC, or a monster, with initiative, hit points, armor class if you track it, and notes for conditions.

How it connects

Remembers the session and scene it began from. A combat scene is your prep; this is the fight itself, live at the table — and when the session ends, it is archived into that session's log.

In the monastery story

The crypt fight — round three, Serra at the top of the order, Maren at four hit points and still refusing to explain himself.
